## Existing facts versus proposals
Repository facts:
- The engine grades per 100 g on the Nutri-Score point tables (`FoodGradeRule.ts` lines 11-39); sugars, saturated fat, sodium and fiber are required (`gradeOfNutrition.ts` lines 8-13, 32-36); a recipe is never graded (`gradeOfCustomFood.ts` line 14-16); meals and days are the calorie-weighted mean of integer scores, rounded half away from zero, 70% coverage (`gradeOfMeal.ts`).
- Every threshold today is strict "greater than" (`nutrientProfileScore.ts` line 4). The draft says "one per threshold passed" without saying whether a value equal to a threshold passes; three calibration foods sit on or within 0.05 of a cut (tofu's micronutrient mean 7.5%, beef's protein 15.1 g, cola's sugars 24-26 g per 100 kcal).
- `RecipeIngredient.totals` is a `NutritionTotals` of kcal, protein, carbs and fat only (`RecipeIngredient.ts` line 13; `NutritionTotals.ts` lines 2-7); `CustomFood.micros` is "none" for a recipe (`CustomFood.ts` line 56). MM-382's choice "a recipe is graded when its ingredients' totals give ... fiber and sodium" has no fiber or sodium to draw on; with sodium required (owner), a recipe stays ungradable unless MM-382 also extends the ingredient.
- The published packs are format 1: no sugars, saturated fat or micronutrients for any pack food (`docs/food-grades.md` lines 58-66; MM-307 "no real build").
- The app's reference amounts are sex- and age-specific RDAs/AIs (`referenceAmount.ts`); the draft uses the FDA Daily Values instead (potassium 4,700 mg, calcium 1,300, iron 18, magnesium 420, zinc 11, vitamin A 900 µg RAE, C 90 mg, D 20 µg, B12 2.4 µg, folate 400 µg DFE: FDA page, current as of 2024-03-05).
- Owner-fixed constraints (MM-380 Decisions; the brief): per-calorie basis; vitamins and minerals count; calories, macros, fiber, sodium required; sugars, saturated fat, micronutrients only when stated; twelve steps; no published model as basis; no moral words.

Everything under Findings and the recommended table is proposal.

## Findings

**NU-1. Energy density per gram, at up to 7 points, decides the bottom half of the scale and moves the same food a letter between its dry and cooked state.** Domain nutrition/measurement. Classification: evidence-supported domain concern. Severity high; confidence high (arithmetic). Dry oats 3.89 kcal/g lose 3 points, cooked oatmeal 0.71 kcal/g lose 0: B- against B. Dry pasta C+ against cooked pasta B-. The pack carries both states (`CatalogFood.preparation`, `pairedFoodId`). The same term puts walnuts (6.54 kcal/g, 6 points) at D+ beside chips and ice cream, almonds at C+ with white rice, and 5 of chips' 5 points against and 5 of peanut butter's 6 are energy density. The literature does support one per-100-g negative term (Drewnowski 2009: negative subscores performed better per 100 g; Darmon 2005: energy density inversely related to nutrient density), so I do not recommend dropping it; I recommend it start where no food is mostly water. **Remediation:** thresholds 3, 4, 5, 6, 7.5 kcal/g (max 5). Moves in the calibration set: salmon B+ to A-, dry oats B- to B (cooked oatmeal B either way), white bread C+ to B-, chips and ice cream D+ to C-, olive oil D to D (with NU-6's cuts), milk chocolate D- to D; walnuts D+ to C-, almonds C+ to B-, dry pasta C+ to B-. Acceptance: a food and its paired cooked/dry state differ by at most one step on the calibration set.

**NU-2. The sugars waiver's gates cut the dairy family in two and strand two whole fruits.** Domain nutrition. Classification: evidence-supported domain concern. Severity high; confidence high. Whole milk has 5.2 g protein per 100 kcal, under the 6 g gate, so its lactose (8.3 g per 100 kcal) costs 2 points and whole milk grades C+ while skim (protein 9.9, waived) grades B+: three steps for the same lactose. Plain whole-milk yogurt (5.7 g) likewise. Grapes (fiber 1.3 g per 100 kcal) and watermelon (1.33) miss the 1.5 g fiber gate: grapes D+ beside chips, watermelon C-. **Remediation:** waive sugars when the food is under 1.5 kcal per g and brings fiber ≥ 1.0 g or protein ≥ 4.5 g per 100 kcal. The energy-density gate is new: it keeps every fresh fruit, vegetable and plain dairy (all under 1.0 kcal/g; avocado and ricotta carry no sugar to waive) and stops the fiber gate from waiving a sugar cereal with added fiber (Froot Loops, 3.75 kcal/g, fiber 2.7 g per 100 kcal, which the draft waives), granola bars, dried fruit, honey and chocolate. Moves: whole milk C+ to B-, grapes D+ to C+, watermelon C- to B-, plain whole-milk yogurt C+ to B-, raisins unchanged D+ (not waived, as the draft). Known leaks, which only an added-sugars field fixes (NU-8): flavoured milks and drinkable yogurts with protein ≥ 4.5 g per 100 kcal, flavoured Greek yogurt (8 g, waived under the draft too), sweetened applesauce and canned fruit in syrup (waived under the draft too), sweetened almond milk when its label states 1 g fiber. The 2025 PREDISE study found replacing total with free sugars changed three models' performance little, which is context for accepting a proxy, not validation of this one.

**NU-3. Per-calorie sodium inverts salt: chips, salted butter and bacon escape, watery and brined foods pay.** Domain nutrition. Classification: evidence-supported domain concern (within an owner-fixed basis). Severity medium; confidence high. Chips 525 mg/100 g = 98 mg per 100 kcal, 0 points; salted butter 643 mg/100 g = 90 per 100 kcal, 0 points; bacon 317 per 100 kcal, 2 points. Celery 571 per 100 kcal, 4 points; Swiss chard and beet greens about 1,100, 7 points; canned green beans about 1,180, 7; pickles about 10,900, the maximum 8. Raw vegetables survive because fiber saturates at 7 and the micronutrient mean is high (celery A-, chard A-), but pickles grade B beside eggs and sauerkraut B+. The first threshold, 120 mg per 100 kcal, is the whole-day sodium DV spread over 2,000 kcal (115), so the scale is internally coherent: a day of chips stays under 2,300 mg. Drewnowski 2009 is the literature point that negatives discriminate better per 100 g. **Remediation within the constraint:** extend the sodium scale with 2,000, 3,000, 5,000 and 8,000 mg per 100 kcal (max 12), which touches only brined foods and condiments: pickles B to C+, sauerkraut B+ to B, soy sauce C+ to C-, a 12 kcal broth C- (unchanged), chard and canned vegetables unchanged. Hand up (decision 3) whether sodium should also count per 100 g above the UK "high" line of 600 mg, which would reach chips and salted butter; the owner fixed per calorie and should see the chips example before it stands.

**NU-4. With format 1 packs, every pack food is graded without sugars, saturated fat or micronutrients, and the letters that result are not the grade the owner asked for.** Domain nutrition/product. Classification: documentation or requirement gap (a consequence of two owner decisions meeting). Severity high; confidence high. Under the old method pack foods showed "?" until format 2 (`docs/food-grades.md` lines 66-70). Under the draft they become gradable at once on protein, fiber, sodium and energy density alone: cola scores 0 and grades C, orange juice B, honey C-, ice cream C+, butter D+; broccoli drops from A+ to A- and tuna from A- to B. A label-typed own food of the same product states sugars and grades differently from the pack's. The popover sentence "sugars and saturated fat weren't counted" is the owner's chosen disclosure but it will not stop cola at C being the most visible nutrition claim in the app on launch day. **Remediation:** record in `decision.md` and the handoff that the grade on pack foods is incomplete until MM-381's format 2 build ships; hand up (decision 1) whether pack-food grades wait for format 2 or show now with the sentence. Acceptance: the register's "How is a food graded?" entry says what a grade without sugars, saturated fat or micronutrients was computed from.

**NU-5. The calibration letters are not reproducible without their inputs; two of the chair's letters I cannot reach.** Domain nutrition/measurement. Classification: documentation or requirement gap. Severity medium; confidence high. On SR Legacy values for 95% lean ground beef (171 kcal, 25.8 g protein, 2.7 g saturated fat, 6.35 mg zinc, 2.6 µg B12) I get 11, A-, where the draft lists B; 85% lean gives about 8, B+. Cheddar (403 kcal, 24.9 g protein, 21 g saturated fat, 621 mg sodium) gives −3, C-, where the draft lists D+; 22.9 g protein gives D+. Both sit on a cut. MM-380's third acceptance criterion requires the inputs listed; MM-382's test will otherwise hold letters nobody can recompute. **Remediation:** the report lists each food's kcal, protein, fiber, sodium, sugars, saturated fat, the ten micronutrients and the source id (`CatalogFood.sourceId`), and the test is built from those rows. My own inputs are in the table below as a starting point and are approximate.

**NU-6. The bottom cut points were drawn to the draft's energy-density scale.** Domain measurement/nutrition. Classification: design improvement. Severity low; confidence medium. With NU-1's scale, concentrated foods lose up to two fewer points, so D+ ≥ −6 and D ≥ −9 leave D+ nearly empty. **Remediation:** D+ ≥ −5, D ≥ −8, D- below (A+ to C- unchanged). With NU-1 to NU-3 together: cola −6 D, olive oil −6 D, milk chocolate −8 D, honey −9 D-, butter −10 D-, raisins and bacon-at-their-values D+ or C-. Measurement should settle the spread; I only ask that the D bands not be empty.

**NU-7. Nuts, seeds and oils: the method cannot see what makes their nutritional case.** Domain nutrition. Classification: evidence-supported domain concern, bounded. Severity medium; confidence high. The ten micronutrients exclude vitamin E (almonds 25.6 mg/100 g, olive oil 14 mg), the fats' kind and omega-3 (walnuts); so walnuts separate from chips by about one point and olive oil ties cola at D. Saturated fat per 100 kcal already reads oils gently (olive oil 1.56 g, 1 point; butter 7.2 g, 6 points), so the gap is in the positives, not the negatives. Reducing SFA lowers combined cardiovascular events by about 17% (Hooper 2020, Cochrane, moderate GRADE), which supports the saturated-fat term's direction; it says nothing about crediting unsaturated fat directly. **Remediation:** not tonight; record the limitation in the register row and the popover ("counts the vitamins and minerals the app tracks"); hand up (decision 4) an unsaturated-fat positive (fat minus saturated fat, per 100 kcal: 3, 5, 7, 9 g, max 4, counted only when saturated fat is stated). Its effect: olive oil D to C-, walnuts C- to C+, almonds B- to B, avocado B to B+, salmon A- unchanged, egg B to B+, and chips also gain 2 (C- to C) because frying oil is unsaturated. I lean against it for a US launch without a trans-fat field, but it is the owner's trade.

**NU-8. Added sugars exist in the sources and would retire most of the waiver.** Domain integrations/nutrition. Classification: documentation or requirement gap. Severity medium; confidence medium (source coverage not measured). USDA Branded carries nutrient 1235 "Sugars, added" for products labelled since 2020; Open Food Facts has `added-sugars_100g` for some products; Foundation/SR/FNDDS do not carry it. **Remediation:** a format 3 column; rule "when a food states added sugars, count added sugars per 100 kcal against it and apply no waiver; otherwise total sugars with the waiver". Referral to integrations for MM-381.

**NU-9. Fortified foods reach the upper bands through the micronutrient mean.** Domain micronutrients. Classification: unverified hypothesis requiring investigation (values from memory). Severity medium. A fortified sugar cereal (Froot Loops, about 375 kcal, 37 g sugars, 10 g added fiber, iron, folic acid, B12 at label levels) scores 6 on micronutrients and grades B under the draft (B+ with the sugars waived) and B under my changes; Cheerios B+; unsweetened fortified almond milk B. Fortified folate and iron are real intake for the US population, so B for a whole-grain fortified cereal is defensible; B for a sugar cereal is not an order a dietitian would accept. Referral to the micronutrients role: whether the micronutrient points are capped (say at 3) when sugars exceed 8 g per 100 kcal, or whether fortification is accepted and disclosed.

**NU-10. Enrichment makes white rice tie brown rice and lifts white bread.** Domain nutrition. Classification: design improvement. Severity low; confidence medium. Enriched white rice (folate 58 µg DFE) and brown rice both reach 1-2 points and grade C+; enriched white bread B- under my changes against whole-wheat B. One step between whole and refined grain is thin but the direction holds. No change proposed; say it in the register row.

**NU-11. Recipes cannot be graded under MM-382's stated rule.** Domain engineering/nutrition. Classification: confirmed requirement gap (MM-382 Decisions versus `RecipeIngredient.ts`). Severity medium; confidence high. Ingredients carry no fiber or sodium; sodium is required. Either MM-382 extends `RecipeIngredient.totals` to carry fiber, sodium and the label micronutrients from the ingredient's origin food, or recipes keep the "?" with a reason naming fiber and sodium.

**NU-12. The ten-kilocalorie floor keeps vegetables gradable and that is right.** Classification: no finding; recorded so the figure is not raised. Iceberg 14, celery 14, cucumber 15, radish 16, tomato 18 kcal/100 g all stay above it; broth, coffee, tea, diet drinks fall under it. A 12 kcal broth grades C- (protein 6 for, sodium 9 against); harmless. The "?" wording must not read as a slight: "Under 10 kcal per 100 g, so there is almost nothing to grade per calorie."

## Acceptance criteria and edge cases
Existing (MM-382): "Per calorie, not per gram" is satisfiable only for protein, fiber and sodium points as written; energy density makes the letter water-sensitive, which NU-1 reduces to at most one step and the scenario should say so. "A recipe" cannot pass as written (NU-11). "Vitamins and minerals" and "Refinements not counted" are fine. Proposed criteria: (1) the dry and cooked state of oats, pasta and rice differ by at most one step; (2) whole, 2% and skim milk grade within two steps and in that order; (3) grapes and watermelon grade no lower than C+; (4) pickles and sauerkraut grade below eggs; (5) a food on a threshold (tofu 7.5%, 95% beef 15.0 g) has its side stated and tested; (6) a pack food without sugars and the same food typed from a label with sugars: the popover of the first names what was not counted; (7) a 9 kcal and an 11 kcal broth: "?" and a grade. Edge cases: partial logging (70% coverage unchanged); an entry's grams unknown (unchanged); fortified foods (NU-9); a food with sugars above carbohydrate (ungradable, not clamped, as before); the 100% cap applied after converting to per 100 kcal (as the draft says; tuna's B12 and spinach's vitamin A depend on it). Vulnerable populations: see Safety.
