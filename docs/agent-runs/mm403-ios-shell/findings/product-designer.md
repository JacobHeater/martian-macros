## Novice mobile experience (when UX/product design is assigned)
Both areas below are **walked** from source (story + component), with no render; the Apple permission sheet itself is outside the app and the story (the fixture permits at once).

**Depth target 1 (walked).** Person: first week on an iPhone, a scale app that writes to Apple Health, wants weigh-ins without typing. Journey: Settings → Apple Health → press Weight → Apple's sheet → back → read the row.
- Step 1, Settings opens. Sees (source): the closed headers of MM-398, except the Apple Health section, which is drawn open (lines 54/114 have no fold) with six rows for the demo's male profile (menstruation only for female, line 111), each two lines of caption-size text, "Not asked yet. <purpose>" with a chevron, and a caption of about six sentences beneath. Must decide which kind to press; the purpose sentence tells them (Weight: "Weigh-ins from your scale, so you need not type them."). Passes for the decision; fails for hierarchy on the whole screen: the one open card (about 6 × 56-64 px of rows plus roughly 7 caption lines, over half of the 915-px viewport) sits between 48-px closed headers and the 24-px gap rule (`ui.css` 1812) makes it the screen's hero. That is PD-1, evidenced by source, not a render.
- Step 2, presses Weight (`allow`, 88-105). Apple's sheet; the person turns Weight on, or not. The platform's step.
- Step 3, back in the app. `permitted` now holds weight whatever they chose (MM-69 decision). Row becomes inert (`opens` false, no `onPress`), no chevron, no check; subtitle "Nothing found in Apple Health yet. If you expected readings, check Health, Sharing, Apps." (lines 22, 18). Snack: the same words with "for Weight" (line 104). "Read from Apple Health now" appears last (137-147). What tells them it worked: the chevron is gone and the snack. What they must interpret: whether "nothing found" means they refused, the scale has not written, or the scale writes elsewhere; the row says where to look but not what to look for (a switch for this app under Weight). Within the owner's bound ("not allowed" is never claimed) the sentence is as honest as it can be; the remaining burden is whether the path matches what they see on their iOS (PD-2) and that the path is said three times in two forms (PD-3). Not a failing step: they know where to go.
- Step 4, after turning sharing on in Health, the app reads on returning to the front (MM-68 progress) and the row shows "1 reading from Bathroom scale." with the check (line 131). Sign: row text plus mark. Passes; no story draws this transition on iOS (PD-5).
Competing groups: none inside the section; the "Read from Apple Health now" row is the only action row once something is asked, correctly last, same as Android. Routine vs uncertainty: the uncertainty ("may be one you did not share") is material and belongs visible; it is currently visible in every iOS state, including those where no row is in that state (PD-3).

**Depth target 2 (walked).** Person: new installation on an iPhone, presses "Not now" out of caution. Render: `TermsNotNowOnIos` (story 43-50, `newInstallScenario`, so no records). Sees: app bar "Terms of use", headline "That is fine" (`--type-title`), one paragraph of three sentences ending ", or leave the app from the Home Screen.", a gap, one full-width Ember button "Read the terms again". With records (`TermsChangedNotNow` on Android; no iOS story) also "Erase all data" as Ember text. Decides among reading again, erasing, leaving. Read again: `setDeclined(false)` returns the terms with both buttons; sign is the terms. Erase: confirm dialog, then the paragraph changes to "Nothing about you has been entered or saved." and the Erase button disappears (31, 70-72, 85); sign is the sentence. Leave: iOS's own gesture; the app can give no sign, which is the platform's. No failing step. Hierarchy: one Ember fill (rule 6); the Android ladder is primary / secondary / text and iOS primary / text, so removing the middle rung leaves the order of weight intact; the leaving instruction belongs in prose, since a non-pressable thing styled as a button would be worse. Card count is not at issue on either screen; the burden I report (PD-1) is from the one open card's size against closed siblings, evidenced by the fold rules in source.

## Findings

**PD-1 — The Apple Health / Health Connect section does not fold like its siblings (both platforms).** Domain: settings / design-system. Classification: confirmed requirement violation (MM-398 Description and AC "Settings opens closed: every section's header shows, closed, and none of their rows"; rule 9 of `design/screen-direction.md`). Severity: high (it is the whole Settings screen's hierarchy on every open, and defeats MM-402's one-screen closed menu). Confidence: high that the source has no fold (`HealthPlatformSection.tsx` 54 and 114 pass only `icon` and `title`; `SettingsScreen.tsx` 216 passes nothing; every sibling spreads `useSettingsSection`, e.g. `RemindersSection.tsx` 25, 65); medium that it is this branch's regression rather than pre-existing: `openSettings.ts` 22 expects Health Connect to fold and be "opened again", and the accordion test omits it from its list (line 28), so the test would not catch it either way. Expected: the Apple Health header closed on opening Settings, opening in place. Observed (source): always open, between closed headers, with the 24-px "opened section" gap (`ui.css` 1812) on both sides. User impact: on an iPhone the largest, wordiest section of Settings is the only one open; the owner's accordion decision is not what the person sees. Remediation: `const fold = useSettingsSection('healthConnect');` and `{...fold}` on both `MmSection` returns; add the section's name (by platform) to `settingsAccordion.test.tsx` line 28's list as the regression test; if it was open on `main` too, it is a bug ticket with that test. Acceptance: Settings opens with the Apple Health header `aria-expanded="false"` and none of its rows; `settingsAt('healthConnect')` still opens it. Verification: source only; the chair can settle regression-vs-pre-existing with `git show 08c11cf:apps/mobile/src/settings/HealthConnectSection.tsx` (or the file's name on main) and by running the accordion test with the Health section added.

**PD-2 — The recovery path "Health, Sharing, Apps" may not be what the person sees, and Apple documents a different one.** Domain: product copy / platform. Classification: unverified hypothesis requiring investigation. Severity: medium (it is the only recovery instruction in the primary failure state, in the row, the snack and the caption). Confidence: medium. Evidence: `HealthPlatformSection.tsx` 18 (`whereToCheck`), 104, 154. Apple's Platform Security guide (2026-01-28) names "Settings > Health > Data Access & Devices" for reviewing and revoking app access; the iPhone User Guide edition I could retrieve (iOS 13) names Health app > profile picture > Privacy > Apps; neither names a "Sharing" tab for app access. The current User Guide page did not resolve past the table of contents, so the label on iOS 15-26 (the brief's floor is iOS 15) is unverified from Apple; non-authoritative search snippets say the Sharing tab's item reads "Apps and Services" on recent iOS. Expected: a path whose last word matches the label on screen at the floor and today. User impact: a novice who has just chosen something on Apple's sheet goes looking for "Apps" and may not find that word. Remediation: the chair checks the iOS 26 simulator's Health app and Settings app and names the path by what is on screen (or the Settings-app path Apple documents as stable); say it in one form everywhere (today the row/snack say "Health, Sharing, Apps." and the caption "Health (Sharing, Apps)"). Acceptance: one path, one form, verified on the simulator and recorded in MM-69. Verification: blocked for me (no device, no shell); bounded chair request below.

**PD-3 — The iOS caption carries more weight than its siblings and repeats the row.** Domain: product design (hierarchy, copy). Classification: design/usability improvement (anti-cruft §3 "one direct sentence beats three", §6 over-explanation; rule 7). Severity: low-medium. Confidence: medium (line counts estimated from tokens, not rendered). Evidence: `HealthPlatformSection.tsx` 149-156: about six sentences on iOS against Reminders' three (`RemindersSection.tsx` 88-91) and Android's about five; the sentence "Apple Health does not tell the app which kinds you allowed, so a kind with nothing found may be one you did not share" says in general what the NothingFound row already says in particular (line 22), and shows in `AppleHealthNoneAsked` and `AppleHealthSomeAsked`, where no row is in that state. Expected: the caption at the weight of its sibling, the uncertainty said once where it applies. Observed: roughly seven caption lines under the card in every iOS state. User impact: the longest text block in Settings sits under the section on an iPhone whatever the state; the "To stop" sentence that matters is last. Remediation (proposal): keep "The app only reads…" and "To stop, take the permission away in Health …"; drop the explanatory sentence from the caption since the row and the snack carry it, or show it only when a permitted kind has nothing found; one form of the path (PD-2). The owner's decision that "the caption says what Apple Health cannot tell the app and where to stop sharing" is met by the row plus the "To stop" sentence; if the owner wants the general sentence kept, that is a legitimate call and this finding is then closed as declined. Acceptance: iOS caption no longer than Android's plus one sentence; `appleHealth.test.tsx` 80-85 updated with the words. Note mm320's UX-9 already found the Android note long and let the words stand on MM-68's authority.

**PD-4 — "Everything allowed is up to date." claims on iOS what the section says Apple Health cannot tell it.** Domain: product copy / health-sync. Classification: confirmed requirement violation of MM-69's owner decision (2026-10-19) "What HealthKit cannot say is not pretended" (the ticket's lines 29-33), mirrored by the brief's proposed criterion. Severity: low (a transient snack, after the person has already connected). Confidence: high. Evidence: `HealthPlatformSection.tsx` 82 is platform-independent; `appleHealth.test.tsx` has no read-now-done case; no `AppleHealth*` story reaches it (`AppleHealthReadFailed` fails the read). Expected on iOS: a sentence in the section's own words, e.g. "Read from Apple Health. Everything asked for is up to date." Observed: "allowed", the word every iOS row avoids. Remediation: branch on `ios` as line 73 does; add a test beside line 56 and a story `AppleHealthReadDone` (the sibling is `HealthConnectReadDone`, 373-383). Acceptance: on iOS no row, snack or caption says a kind is "allowed". Verification: source only.

**PD-5 — iOS states with words of their own have no story.** Domain: design register (MM-293, MM-292). Classification: documentation or requirement gap. Severity: low. Confidence: high. Evidence: the five `AppleHealth*` stories (686-741) match MM-69's list; none presses a kind row, so the owner cannot see in Storybook: a kind asked and read ("Weight read from Apple Health.", line 104, sibling `HealthConnectAllowing` 360-370); asked with nothing found, as a snack (line 104); asked but the read failed ("Weight asked for, but Apple Health could not be read just now…", line 73, sibling UX-11 test); Read now done (line 82, PD-4); every kind asked (sibling `HealthConnectAllAllowed`). `npm run unseen` checks markup, not words, so nothing fails. Remediation: add the four or five stories with `onIos(healthPlatform(...))` and a `walk`. Acceptance: each iOS-only sentence in `HealthPlatformSection.tsx` is reachable from a story.

**PD-6 — "phone" and "device" on the same platform.** Domain: product copy. Classification: design/usability improvement. Severity: low. Confidence: medium. Evidence: on iOS the Health section says "device" (lines 58, 150, the story comment at 686 citing iPad); `TermsGuardScreen.tsx` 57-58 and 71 say "phone"; `termsOfUse.ts` says "phone" throughout; `SettingsScreen.tsx` 72-73 says "device" on both platforms. Pre-existing across the app, made visible by the iOS branch's deliberate "device". Remediation: one word per platform, by whether iPad is a target (MM-403 says iPhone, portrait); if not, "phone" everywhere on iOS too. Acceptance: a test that the declined page and the Health caption use the same word on one platform.

**PD-7 — Consider a state word on asked rows, as on Android.** Domain: product copy / hierarchy. Classification: design/usability improvement. Severity: low. Confidence: low-medium (judgment). Evidence: Android rows all open with a state word ("Allowed." / "Not allowed.", lines 22, 27, 127); iOS unasked rows open "Not asked yet." but asked rows open with the count or "Nothing found…" and no state word. "Asked. 5 readings from Watch." / "Asked. Nothing found in Apple Health yet. …" would keep the parallel grammar, say the one thing the app does know (the sheet was shown), and give the no-mark row a word in place of the mark. Not a violation; preference against the sibling's grammar.

**Assessed, no finding:** the check mark on iOS. Three trailing treatments carry three states (chevron: not asked, pressable; nothing: asked, nothing found, inert; check: readings present, inert). The check standing only for a kind that was read is the honest choice; a mark for "asked" would claim what the sheet did not say. It is redundant with the subtitle's count exactly as the Android check is with "Allowed." (mm320 UX-7 hid it from the reader on that ground), so the sibling pattern holds. The NothingFound subtitle (two sentences, about 87 characters) wraps to about two caption lines, as the purpose subtitles do, so row heights stay even; its length is not a hierarchy break. The declined terms page: no finding beyond PD-6; one Ember fill, prose for what the platform forbids as a button, "Home Screen" in Apple's own capitalization.
