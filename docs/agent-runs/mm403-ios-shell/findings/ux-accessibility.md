## Findings
Severity is impact-based; confidence says why. None is blocking.

**F1 — The recovery path "Health, Sharing, Apps" is not the route Apple documents, and is written two ways.** Domain: ux-accessibility (wording), react-capacitor for the alternative. Class: **usability problem found by inspection** (step 4b). Severity **medium**: met by everyone who refuses or whose scale has not written, each time; passable (the Health app lists apps under the profile picture in every version I could evidence), but a novice following the exact words on a phone matching Apple's current article taps the Sharing tab and finds people and providers. Confidence **medium**: Apple's current support article (published 2026-03-10) gives Summary tab → profile picture → "Under Privacy, tap Apps"; Apple's Health privacy page (2026-09-14) gives "Settings > Health"; Apple's developer page says "either the Settings or the Health app"; the "Sharing" tab route with "Apps and Services" (iOS 16-18) and "Apps" (iOS 15) comes only from third-party guides and search snippets, since Apple's User Guide pages would not render for me. The app's floor is iOS 15, so the row's words may match iOS 15 exactly and iOS 16-18 nearly. Where: `HealthPlatformSection.tsx:18` (row and snack), `:154` (caption, "Health (Sharing, Apps)"); tests `appleHealth.test.tsx:75-76, 84`; MM-69 line 33 names the path, so the change is to the ticket too. Expected: a route that holds on every supported iOS and is written once. Observed: one route, two notations. User impact: the one step of the task that needs recovery sends the person to a tab whose purpose Apple describes as sharing with people. Remediation (proposal): say the route Apple documents and the one that does not move between versions, once, in the same words in row, snack and caption, for example "check the Health app: your picture, Privacy, Apps" with "or Settings, Privacy & Security, Health" in the caption; or, better, ask react-capacitor whether the plugin can open the app's own page in Settings (`UIApplication.openSettingsURLString`) so the row offers "Open Settings" and the words need not name a path at all. Also cover the person who refused and changed her mind: "If you expected readings, or want to allow it now, …" (the row today speaks only to the expecting case; MM-68 line 44 asks that the app "says how to reconnect"). Acceptance (proposed): one notation of the route in row, snack and caption; the route checked on the owner's phone at acceptance (its iOS version recorded); the words test updated. Verification: source inspection and retrieved Apple pages; not tried on a device.

**F2 — ", or leave the app from the Home Screen" can be read as removing the app.** Domain: ux-accessibility. Class: **usability problem found by inspection** (walk 2, step 2). Severity **medium**: met every time the page is met (rare), nothing forces the misreading and nothing on the page is destructive, but iOS offers "Remove from Home Screen" and "Delete App" on a long press, and a person with records who deletes loses them (terms §10). Confidence **medium**: analytical, from the sentence's grammar (one leaves *to* the Home Screen, not *from* it) and the existence of the iOS menu item; no user observed. Where: `TermsGuardScreen.tsx:73-74`; test `termsOfUse.test.tsx:87`; story `FirstRun.stories.tsx:45`; MM-403 line 99 ("it says the app can be left from the Home Screen") is where the requirement's words would change too. Expected: an unmistakable route out that also says what happens next. Observed: an ambiguous phrase and silence on the next launch. Remediation (proposal): "You can read the terms again whenever you like, or go back to the Home Screen; the app will ask again when you open it." (Android's page would gain the second clause too, if the chair wants the siblings to match.) Acceptance (proposed): the sentence names going to the Home Screen as the action and says the terms return on the next launch; the iOS test asserts it. Verification: source inspection only.

**F3 — "Read from Apple Health. Everything allowed is up to date." says "allowed" on the platform where the app has just said it cannot know.** Domain: ux-accessibility. Class: **design/usability improvement**. Severity **low**: a one-word inconsistency met only after pressing read-now; in step 5 it sits beside a row saying nothing was found. Confidence high (source). Where: `HealthPlatformSection.tsx:82`; Android sibling test `healthConnect.test.tsx:153`. Remediation: on iOS "Read from Apple Health. Everything asked for is up to date." (or "Everything is up to date."). Acceptance: no iOS string says "allowed" of a kind nothing was read from (extends the brief's proposed criterion to snacks). Verification: source inspection.

Confidence **medium**: `HealthPlatformSection.tsx:54, 114` pass no fold props while `RemindersSection.tsx:25` uses `useSettingsSection('reminders')`; `'healthConnect'` exists as a `SettingsSectionId` and the stories pass `settingsAt('healthConnect')` (`Settings.stories.tsx:691`), which today changes nothing. Acceptance: either the section folds like Reminders, or the ticket gives the reason. Verification: source inspection.

**F5 — "device" and "phone" for the same thing on iOS.** Domain: ux-accessibility; product-designer may weigh in. Class: **design/usability improvement**. Severity **low**. Confidence high. Where: section says "device" (`HealthPlatformSection.tsx:58, 150`), the story's rationale is an iPad (`Settings.stories.tsx:686`), while the terms (`termsOfUse.ts:87, 90, 100-102`), the declined page (`TermsGuardScreen.tsx:71`) and its erase dialog (`:57-58`) say "phone"; Settings' own erase dialog says "device" (`SettingsScreen.tsx:72`). Remediation: a sentence in MM-69's UI/UX section that the sheet itself is Apple's and both of its outcomes are drawn, so the next reviewer does not look for it. Verification: source inspection of the stories.
