# Evidence register

Every constant and rule that decides something about a user's body or safety,
with where it comes from and how good that evidence is. A test
(`apps/mobile/test/engine/evidenceRegister.test.ts`) fails `mm check` when a
constant or rule has no row here, or a row names something that no longer
exists. The app's **How this works** screen is generated from this file.

## Grades

Defined once, used in tickets, in this file and in the app's own words.

| Grade | In the app | Means |
|---|---|---|
| strong | Well established | Consistent meta-analyses, consensus statements, or established physiology. |
| moderate | Reasonably supported | A good meta-analysis with inconsistency, or several trials, or strong evidence in a population different from ours. |
| emerging | Early evidence | One or a few small studies. |
| judgement | Our judgement | Expert practice or product reasoning with no direct trial. |

A judgement row is not a failure: many product decisions cannot be anything
else. The failure is a judgement presented as science. **Explanations elsewhere
may not claim more than the row's grade.**

## About the sources

The citations below are the ones recorded in the code and in the requirement
tickets, which were gathered by literature search and, in some cases, from
secondary summaries. **The Checked column is No until someone has read the
primary source and confirmed that it says what the row says.** A row is not
done until then, and the professional review (MM-29) is recorded against rows,
not against the app as a whole. No row has been reviewed by a professional.

Population matters: much of the literature is young, male and trained, or has
obesity and is untrained. Where a row is applied beyond its population, that is
an extrapolation and the row says so.

## Questions

Plain-language entries for the app, grouped by question. Each lists the rows it
draws on.

### How are my calories set?

Your target is your estimated daily energy use, adjusted for the pace of
weight change you chose. The pace is limited by your body fat, because faster
loss costs more muscle in lean people. The energy in a kilogram of body weight
depends on how much of it is fat and how much is lean tissue, which the app
estimates from your body fat. The target never goes below a floor set by your
resting energy and, for lean people, by the energy needed to support the body.

Rows: computeTargets.weeklyRate, SafetyBounds.maxWeeklyLossFraction, SafetyBounds.maxSurplusKcal, SafetyBounds.maxSurplusFractionOfExpenditure, SafetyBounds.maxSurplusFractionForNovices, SafetyBounds.calorieFloorKcal, SafetyBounds.absoluteFloorKcal, SafetyBounds.energyAvailabilityThresholdPercent, SafetyBounds.minEnergyAvailabilityKcalPerKgFfm, fatMassKcalPerKg, fatFreeMassKcalPerKg, forbesConstantKg, leanFractionOfChange

### How is my protein target chosen?

Protein is set in grams from your body weight, using a reference weight that
counts only part of any weight above a body mass index of 25, so heavier people
are not given very large targets. You see both a minimum and a target; the
minimum depends on your goal and training, and the target rises in a lean
deficit. If you have chronic kidney disease, both are capped and you are told
to confirm them with your clinician.

Rows: SafetyBounds.proteinRangeG, SafetyBounds.proteinTargetsG, SafetyBounds.referenceWeightKg, SafetyBounds.excessWeightFraction, SafetyBounds.minFatG, computeTargets.macroSplit

### How does the app know how much energy I use?

At first it estimates it from your size, age and sex, then from how active your
day is and how often you train. After about two weeks of logging and weighing it
replaces that estimate with a measurement: the food you logged, less the energy
in the weight you gained or lost. The measurement is more reliable the more
fully you log and the more regularly you weigh in.

### How is my trend weight worked out?

Your scale weight moves by a kilogram or two from day to day for reasons that
are not body tissue. The trend is a smoothed estimate that separates the slow
change from that noise, and it shows how uncertain it is. A reading that is far
out of line is set aside.

Rows: WeightTrendModel.relativeWaterSigma, WeightTrendModel.waterPersistence, WeightTrendModel.relativeScaleSigma, WeightTrendModel.levelProcessSigmaKg, WeightTrendModel.slopeProcessSigmaKgPerDay, WeightTrendModel.initialSlopeSigmaKgPerDay, WeightTrendModel.outlierSigmas, WeightTrendModel.maxConsecutiveRejections, settlingShiftFraction, settlingSlopeShiftFraction

### Why did the scale jump?

A weigh-in well above or below your trend is usually water, food still being
digested, or stored carbohydrate, not fat: gaining a kilogram of fat in a day
would take many thousands of calories over what you use. When a reading is far
enough from the trend, the app looks through the last two days of your own log
for the likely causes it knows about: a weight event you noted, a day of much
more carbohydrate or sodium than usual, calories outside the target band,
alcohol, a workout, the days around a period start, or a recent change of
targets. It names what it finds as likely, never certain, and says what the
difference would have had to cost to be fat.

### Why do my targets change slowly?

Targets change at most once a week, by a small step, so one unusual week cannot
swing them. A step limit also stops a run of poorly logged days from pushing
targets down. Safety rules can override the limit in one direction: when you are
losing faster than the app aims for, calories go back up at once.

Rows: SafetyBounds.maxWeeklyTargetChangeKcal, SafetyBounds.maxWeeklyTargetChangeFraction, SafetyBounds.maxWeeklyTargetChange, checkInIntervalDays, safetyRaiseLookbackDays, safetyRaiseMinWeighIns, safetyRaiseSigmas, safetyRaiseCapKcal

### How sure is my coach?

Confidence is shown as Learning, Fair or Good, based on the weakest of the
expenditure estimate, recent usable food days, weigh-ins and stability. It is
not a score. At Learning, ordinary target changes wait while the coach learns;
the Coach screen shows the part that needs attention and one next step.

Rows: assessCoachConfidence, confidenceGoodEstimateSigmaKcal, confidenceLearningEstimateSigmaKcal, confidenceGoodFoodDays, confidenceMinimumFoodDays, confidenceGoodWeighInDays, confidenceMinimumWeighInDays, confidenceStabilityDays

### When does the app slow or stop a deficit?

A deficit is not offered below a body mass index of 18.5, and loss is limited to
the gentlest pace below 20. After twelve unbroken weeks of deficit the app offers
a two-week break at maintenance, and offers one whenever your weekly check-ins
say recovery is failing; at twenty weeks it plans the break itself. A break is a
rest from dieting that many people find makes the next weeks easier; the app does
not claim it does anything to your metabolism. Goals are also limited by your
health check.

Rows: underweightBmi, lowWeightCautionBmi, lowWeightMaxLossFraction, DietBreakRule.offerFromWeeks, DietBreakRule.forceFromWeeks, DietBreakRule.breakWeeks, DietBreakRule.resetDays

### How sure is the app about my body fat?

Body fat is estimated from height, weight, age and sex unless you give your own
figure. Once you have measured your waist a few times in four weeks, a second
formula from your height and waist joins the first and the range narrows a
little; it stays a range, because both formulas can be off by several points
for the same build. A smart scale's body-fat readings join once they span two
weeks: the middle of the last two weeks, updated once a week, so an overnight
swing moves nothing. Averaging removes a scale's day-to-day scatter, not its
fixed bias, so the range narrows to a few points and no further. Each scale has
its own bias: a new scale is learned against the one before it over its first
week, so switching scales is not read as a change in your body. Where a safety
limit depends on it, the app takes the
cautious side when you could plausibly be on it, and does not switch back and
forth on a small change.

### How does the app plan the phases ahead?

The Coach shows the phase you are in, how long it has run and, at your current
pace, about when it ends. A cut ends once the lower end of your body-fat range
reaches its goal, after sixteen weeks in a deficit, or when progress has
stalled with the target already at the lowest the app sets; a lean gain when
the lean-gain guardrails say so (the upper end of the body-fat range at its
line, the waist up 5%, or twenty-six weeks), so the two never disagree. Then
the app recommends what comes next, with the reason: a hold at maintenance,
then a lean gain if you are at the cut's goal or another cut if not. Body fat
is read as a range, never as one figure (a figure you entered counts as
exact), and nothing changes until you choose.
The weeks and lines are the app's judgement.

Rows: PhaseRule.longestCutWeeks, PhaseRule.lowGoalHoldWeeks, PhaseRule.steadyPaceSigmas, PhaseRule.cutGoalPercent, LeanGainRule.stopBodyFatPercent, gainLineBodyFatPercent, LeanGainRule.stopWaistRiseFraction, LeanGainRule.stopWeeks, planPhases, SafetyBounds.goalBodyFatFloorsPercent, SafetyBounds.checkGoalBodyFat

### When does the app ask about my health?

The health check is repeated every 90 days and before you switch to a deficit.
You can defer it once; after a second skip, deficit coaching pauses at
maintenance until you confirm your answers.

Rows: UserSetup.healthCheckIntervalDays, CoachingPolicy.seniorMaxWeeklyLossFraction, CoachingPolicy.seniorMinimumProteinGPerKgReferenceWeight, CoachingPolicy.insulinUnconfirmedMaxWeeklyLossFraction, CoachingPolicy.targetsAllowed

### How does sleep come into it?

Your nights, typed or read from your health platform, are averaged over the
last fourteen. When that average is under six hours across at least seven
recorded nights, the coach names it: in a deficit, short sleep appears to shift
what is lost away from fat and toward lean tissue, and hunger runs higher. It
offers the gentler pace and changes nothing until you choose. A diagnosed sleep
disorder is a caution and a pointer to your clinician, and changes no number.

Rows: SleepRule.shortSleepHours, SleepRule.windowNights, SleepRule.minimumNights, Caution.sleepDisorder

### How is a food graded?

Every food gets a grade from A+ to D- for the food itself, from what it
gives per calorie: the same grade for everyone, whatever the amount eaten.
Protein, fiber and the vitamins and minerals the app tracks count for a
food; sodium, sugars, saturated fat and calories per gram count against it.
The method is the app's own, reviewed by the panel's nutrition,
micronutrients and measurement roles and held to a calibration set of
reference foods; every figure below is a judgement. A food is graded when it
states calories, the macros, fiber and sodium; sugars, saturated fat and the
vitamins and minerals count when stated, and the grade's popover says when
they were not; a fiber that is not stated is counted as none, which can only
hold the letter down. A fried, battered or breaded food, by the words of its
name, or a food that is mostly fat and sugar with little that counts for it,
grades no better than C-, and the popover says so: a nutrient table cannot
see a fryer, and per calorie a battered, sauced dish can look like a grilled
one. A step is about the resolution of the inputs: a label may be
20% off and comply, so neighbouring letters are not a ranking to act on. The
grade does not reflect a clinician's restrictions. A meal's or a day's grade
counts its foods by their calories. No grade for an alcoholic drink, a food
with almost no calories, an estimate, a recipe, or a food whose grams or
millilitres are not known.

### How far can a food's numbers be trusted?

Food labels are allowed to be off: in the United States calories and several
nutrients may run up to 20% over the label before a product is misbranded,
and measured foods do run over on average. The coach is built to cope with
that as long as you log the same way from week to week, so close is as good
as exact. Each food says where its numbers came from and marks one of three
things: Reference (analysed by USDA), Label (a maker's label, or a community
copy of one that is complete and consistent), or Check this, with the
reason: two sources disagree, its calories and macros only just agree, it
leaves out fiber or sodium, or it has not changed in five years. Nothing is
called verified, because nobody checks a food. "Fix this food" keeps a
corrected copy of your own.

Rows: CommunityReviewRule.nearToleranceFraction, CommunityReviewRule.nearToleranceKcal, CommunityReviewRule.staleSeconds

### How are foods ranked?

Foods from analysed sources list first (USDA Foundation, then SR Legacy,
then the survey foods as eaten), then manufacturer labels, then community
entries; within a source, a record that states more of its nutrients lists
first, then the closer match to what you typed. Your own and recent foods
always list above the database. The same score decides which record a
scanned barcode shows when two sources carry it. It is the app's judgement
of each source, not a check of any food, and it is never shown beside one.

Rows: sourceReliability, SourceReliabilityRule.bases, SourceReliabilityRule.basesByTier, SourceReliabilityRule.disagreementPenalty, SourceReliabilityRule.fiberAndSodiumBonus, SourceReliabilityRule.sugarsAndSaturatedFatBonus

### What does the coach read in my data?

The coach looks for patterns over the span you choose, and says one only
when two things hold: the pattern is clear against your own day-to-day
spread, and it is big enough to matter. Days you did not log are never
counted as zero, and too few logged days means the coach waits. Each food
day is read against the targets in force that day. Training is read in
weeks, since rest days are real. The thresholds are set so that, in
simulation, someone whose eating and training do not change sees at most
about one new finding a month. Most of the thresholds are the app's own
judgement.

Rows: TrendRule.sigmaScale, TrendRule.coverageMinimum, TrendRule.levelAgainstTarget, TrendRule.drift, TrendRule.shift, TrendRule.longRun, TrendRule.recentAgainstBaseline, TrendRule.newEvaluations, TrendTier, foodTrendSignals, foodSignal.proteinMinimum, foodSignal.fatAgainstTarget, foodSignal.calorieDensity, foodSignal.calories, findTrade, analyseFoodDay, trainingSignal.trainingDropped, trainingSignal.setsPerMuscle, trainingSignal.strengthOnFatLoss, trainingSignal.waterOnTrainingDays, coachFindingWords
