# Epic: Food logging

## Context
The coach is only as good as the food log, and food logging is where calorie apps lose people. Advanced users weigh every gram; beginners
give up when a search for "grilled chicken breast" returns twelve conflicting entries. Planning set two principles:

- **Most logging is repetition.** People eat perhaps thirty to fifty foods over and over. The main path is "what I ate before", not search.
- **Consistency beats accuracy.** The adaptive estimate absorbs a steady logging bias (MM-23), so a rough method used the same way every
  day is worth more than a precise one used sometimes.

## Narrative
What exists is the floor: type a food's macros (MM-38), re-log a recent food in one tap (MM-39), see the day against targets (MM-41), and
say whether the day is complete (MM-40).

What turns it into a product is finding foods without typing numbers: search (MM-42), barcode (MM-43), and the nutrition label when a
barcode is unknown (MM-44), all resting on the food database (MM-50). Then the things that make repetition fast: saved foods and recipes
(MM-45), copying a meal or a day (MM-47), and editing an entry (MM-48). Then the two ends of the audience: hand portions for people who will
not weigh (MM-46), and a detail setting for people who want fiber, net carbs and more (MM-49).

Food logging is free forever (MM-85).

## Acceptance Criteria (narrative)
The Epic is done when a user can log a typical day in under two minutes without typing a nutrition number, by some mix of recents, search
and barcode; when a food they cannot find can still be logged in a few seconds without corrupting their totals; and when a beginner using
hand portions and an expert using a food scale both get sound guidance from the coach.
